{\small YAOHAREE LAHTEE}

\vspace{0.18in}

Consider the unforced periodic three-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes equation after
finite cubic Fourier--Galerkin truncation.  This note gives exact finite-dimensional
observability results at the first two cubic resolutions.  For $N=1$, total retained kinetic
energy and its first $48$ dynamical derivatives have generic local rank $49$ in a
$52$-dimensional state space.  For $N=2$, the same scalar reader and its first $244$
derivatives have generic local rank $245$ in a $248$-dimensional state space.  In both cases
the rank equals the largest value allowed by spatial-translation symmetry and is attained at
the earliest order allowed by scalar output dimension.  Exact modular certificates establish
the finite ranks, and a scaling conjugacy transfers each result to every viscosity $\nu>0$.
No continuum regularity claim is made.

\section*{1. Finite system and universal ceiling}

For $N\ge1$, retain
\begin{equation}
K_N=\{-N,\ldots,N\}^3\setminus\{0\}.
\end{equation}
Reality and incompressibility leave the real phase-space dimension
\begin{equation}
\boxed{d_N=2\bigl((2N+1)^3-1\bigr).}
\end{equation}
Write
\begin{equation}
\dot x=F_\nu(x)=\nu Ax+B(x,x),\qquad \nu>0,
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the projected quadratic Navier--Stokes interaction.  Let
\begin{equation}
E(x)=\frac12\sum_{k\in K_N}|\uHat_k|^2
\end{equation}
be total retained kinetic energy, and define its Lie jet
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_R=(E,\Lie_{F_\nu}E,\ldots,\Lie_{F_\nu}^{R}E).
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal E_R$ has $R+1$ scalar coordinates,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{rank}D\mathcal E_R\le R+1.
\end{equation}
Spatial translation by $a\in\T^3$ acts by
\begin{equation}
(T_a\uHat)_k=e^{ik\cdot a}\uHat_k.
\end{equation}
The Galerkin dynamics is translation equivariant and $E$ is translation invariant.  Thus
every component of $\mathcal E_R$ is constant on translation orbits.  At a generic state the
orbit has dimension three, so
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{rank}D\mathcal E_R\le d_N-3.
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}D\mathcal E_R\le\min(R+1,d_N-3).}
\end{equation}
In particular, the symmetry-limited maximum $d_N-3$ is impossible before
\begin{equation}
\boxed{R=d_N-4.}
\end{equation}

\section*{2. Exact finite certificates at $N=1$ and $N=2$}

The executable certificates propagate formal Taylor coefficients of the Galerkin flow and its
tangent system.  For polynomial $F$ and $h$,
\begin{equation}
h(\Phi_t x)=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{t^r}{r!}\Lie_F^rh(x).
\end{equation}
Therefore Taylor-coefficient and Lie-derivative Jacobians have the same rank through order
$R$ over $\F_p$ whenever $p>R$.

For $N=1$, the retained set contains $26$ nonzero modes and $d_1=52$.  The existing exact
checker uses $p=1{,}000{,}003$ and $\nu_0=1/200$ and gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\F_p}D\mathcal E_R=\min(R+1,49),\qquad 0\le R\le48.}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\F_p}D\mathcal E_{48}=49.}
\end{equation}

For $N=2$, there are $124$ nonzero modes and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{d_2=248.}
\end{equation}
The second certificate uses $p=251>244$ and $\nu_0=1/200$.  A concrete finite-field state
$x_0\in\F_{251}^{248}$ and a concrete projection
$P\in\F_{251}^{248\times245}$ are fixed by the recorded seeds.  The checker forms the exact
projected Jacobian
\begin{equation}
\mathcal O_{244}=D\mathcal E_{244}(x_0)P\in\F_{251}^{245\times245}
\end{equation}
and obtains
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\F_{251}}\mathcal O_{244}=245.}
\end{equation}
Since rank cannot increase under right multiplication,
\begin{equation}
245\le \operatorname{rank}_{\F_{251}}D\mathcal E_{244}(x_0)\le245,
\end{equation}
where the last inequality is the translation ceiling.  Hence equality holds.  Moreover, the
$245$ rows of $\mathcal O_{244}$ are linearly independent, so every prefix has its maximal
possible row rank:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\F_{251}}D\mathcal E_R=R+1,\qquad0\le R\le244.}
\end{equation}
The recorded milestones include
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
R&10&30&60&120&180&220&243&244\\ \hline
\operatorname{rank}&11&31&61&121&181&221&244&245.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
These are exact finite-field ranks, not floating-point rank estimates.

A nonzero maximal minor at a good prime implies that the corresponding characteristic-zero
rational minor polynomial is not identically zero.  Combining this lower bound with (9) gives
the generic characteristic-zero rank.

\section*{3. Two-resolution theorem and viscosity universality}

\begin{theorem}[Optimal scalar-energy observability at two resolutions]
For $N\in\{1,2\}$ and every $\nu>0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\rankgen D\mathcal E_{d_N-4}=d_N-3.}
\end{equation}
More explicitly,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
N=1:\quad 52\longrightarrow49,\qquad R_{\min}=48,
}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
N=2:\quad 248\longrightarrow245,\qquad R_{\min}=244.
}
\end{equation}
In each case the scalar energy jet reaches the translation-limited generic local rank ceiling
at the earliest mathematically possible derivative order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The certificates above prove the claimed generic characteristic-zero ranks at
$\nu_0=1/200$.  To transfer them to arbitrary positive viscosity, set
$x(t)=\nu y(s)$ and $s=\nu t$.  Quadratic homogeneity gives
\begin{equation}
F_\nu(\nu y)=\nu^2F_1(y).
\end{equation}
Since $E$ is quadratic,
\begin{equation}
\Lie_{F_\nu}^rE(\nu y)=\nu^{r+2}\Lie_{F_1}^rE(y),
\qquad
D_x\Lie_{F_\nu}^rE(\nu y)=\nu^{r+1}D_y\Lie_{F_1}^rE(y).
\end{equation}
For $\nu>0$ these are invertible state and row scalings, so the jet ranks are viscosity
independent.  Optimality follows from (9)--(10).
\end{proof}

The endpoint $\nu=0$ is different: total kinetic energy is conserved for the finite Euler
system, so the scalar energy jet collapses to $(E,0,0,\ldots)$.

\section*{4. Local geometry and resolution pattern}

For $N=1,2$, generic states have trivial continuous translation stabilizer; for example,
nonzero coefficients at the three axial modes $e_1,e_2,e_3$ force a fixing translation to
satisfy $e^{ia_1}=e^{ia_2}=e^{ia_3}=1$.  Hence the generic local quotient by $\T^3$ has
dimension $d_N-3$.

\begin{corollary}[Local completeness modulo translation]
For $N\in\{1,2\}$, every $\nu>0$, and generic regular states,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\ker D\mathcal E_{d_N-4}(x)=T_x(\T^3\!\cdot x).
}
\end{equation}
Thus the only generic infinitesimally unobservable directions at the saturated order are the
three spatial translations.
\end{corollary}

The two exact resolution points are therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
(d_N,R_{\min},\operatorname{rank})=(52,48,49),\ (248,244,245).
}
\end{equation}
They motivate, but do not prove, the following extension.

\begin{conjecture}[Resolution-indexed energy-readout saturation]
For every finite cubic truncation $K_N$ and every $\nu>0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\rankgen D\mathcal E_{d_N-4}=d_N-3.}
\end{equation}
\end{conjecture}

The theorem proves the conjecture only for $N=1$ and $N=2$.  No inference to all $N$ or to
the continuum equation is made.

\section*{5. Scope and verification record}

The result is a finite-dimensional observability theorem.  It does not prove continuum
regularity or singularity formation, global state reconstruction, stable recovery from noisy
high-order derivatives, or computational speed-up.  Its precise content is that a single
physically natural scalar observable reaches the maximal generic local information dimension
allowed by translation symmetry, at the earliest scalar-jet order, at two distinct cubic
Fourier--Galerkin resolutions.

## 2. Fourier-plane closure

Let `m in Z^3\{0}` be primitive and define the lattice plane

\[
\Pi_m=\{k\in\mathbb Z^3:m\cdot k=0\}.
\]

If

\[
p,q\in\Pi_m,
\]

then

\[
m\cdot(p+q)=m\cdot p+m\cdot q=0,
\]

so

\[
p+q\in\Pi_m.
\]

The Fourier form of the NSE nonlinearity couples only wavevectors satisfying `p+q=k`.  The Leray projection acts on the velocity polarization at the same output wavevector `k`; it does not change `k`.  Viscosity is diagonal in `k`.

Hence the set of vector fields with Fourier support in `Pi_m` is invariant under the NSE vector field.  For a classical/mild solution in its uniqueness class, initial support in `Pi_m` remains in `Pi_m`.

## 3. Physical-space reduction

Let

\[
e_3=\frac{m}{|m|}
\]

and let `Pi=e_3^perp`.  Fourier support in `Pi_m` is equivalent to

\[
\partial_{e_3}u=0.
\]

Decompose

\[
u=v+w e_3,
\qquad v\in\Pi.
\]

Because `partial_{e3}=0`, incompressibility gives

\[
\operatorname{div}_{\Pi}v=0.
\]

Moreover

\[
u\cdot\nabla
=v\cdot\nabla_{\Pi}+w\partial_{e_3}
=v\cdot\nabla_{\Pi}.
\]

Projecting the NSE onto the plane and normal direction gives

\[
\boxed{
\partial_t v+v\cdot\nabla_{\Pi}v+\nabla_{\Pi}p
=\nu\Delta_{\Pi}v,
\qquad
\operatorname{div}_{\Pi}v=0,
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\partial_t w+v\cdot\nabla_{\Pi}w
=\nu\Delta_{\Pi}w.
}
\]

Thus `v` is a two-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes solution on the induced flat two-torus, and `w` is an advected-diffused scalar driven by `v`.

## 4. Regularity of the branch

Classical 2D incompressible NSE on a compact flat two-torus is globally regular for smooth data.  Once `v` is smooth, the scalar equation for `w` is linear uniformly parabolic with smooth divergence-free drift.  Standard energy/derivative estimates therefore propagate smoothness of `w` for every finite time.

Consequently an exactly plane-supported smooth solution cannot develop a three-dimensional NSE singularity.
